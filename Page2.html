First step

The first step is the switch connecting to the internet. This means that the switch connects via radio waves that alternate in lengths. It connects via your home router. This was provided via your ISP or internet service provider.

Second step

The switch uses failsafe to save backups of your character data in case of interference. The switch uses failsafe methods to save a backup of your character data in case of interrupted connection.

Third step

The switch sends your character data in the form of packets using radio waves from routers. These packets use NAT to load on a few more bits, called a port number, so the packets can be routed to the correct place.

IP and TCP

Part of step 3 includes the use of IP ,or internet protocol, and tcp, or transmission control protocol, to route your character data correctly. The first step involves TCP where you character data is broken down into smaller sections that are labeled so they can be reassembled. The second step is IP. IP puts its own header on your character data and also checks the IP of the destination. Lastly, It helps route the package to its destination.

Fouth step

The fourth step involves the routing of your character data. Your character data is routed through routers that are in between your computer and your desired location. The routers can find the cheapest or easiest way to route your character data

The Fifth step/Final step

The other person's switch will also use TCP and IP to confirm your information. You and your friend can now crossplay between each other's switches!
